<Update label="September 18, 2026">

  <Frame>
    ![Message reactions](/assets/18-09-2026-changelog.png)

  ## Message reactions

  You can now **react to a message** and remove that reaction from the Heyy inbox on **WhatsApp**, **Instagram**, and **Messenger**. Your customers already send 👍, ❤️, and 😂 on those apps. Until now, your team could not even see them.

  That’s fixed. A quick reaction is perfect for “got it” or “love this” without another full reply cluttering the thread.
</Update>

<Update label="September 15, 2026">

  <Frame>
    ![Multi-select contact attribute](/assets/15-09-2026-changelog.png)
  </Frame>

  ## Multi-select contact attribute

  Contact attributes can now be **multi-select**. Add your options (with colors, same as single-select), then pick as many as you need on the contact card, in bulk edit, or in automations. Languages, products, markets, interests. All of it.

  Filters, conditions, and set/clear attribute steps handle multiple values too, so your next campaign can target everyone who tagged “skincare” without forcing them into one box. See [Custom attributes](/help/contacts/mini-crm/custom-attributes-as-columns).
</Update>

<Update label="September 13, 2026">

  <Frame>
    ![Campaigns 2.0](/assets/13-09-2026-changelog.png)
  </Frame>

  ## Campaigns 2.0

  **Campaigns 2.0** brings the full flow into one place: build, review, send, and see what happened. No more jumping between automations, contacts just to send a campaign. You walk through content, audience, and review on WhatsApp or SMS. Each campaign is an automation with a new **Campaign message** trigger that sets what people get and what happens next (reply, button, list, link, or nothing).

  Pick your audience three ways: **static** (hand-picked contacts), **dynamic** (filters define the list), or **file** (upload a CSV or Excel; contacts stay in your CRM). Before you hit send, you’ll see estimated credits, audience size, and whether everything is actually ready, including Meta template status.

  After the send goes out, check the funnel, engagement, replies, and delivery issues in the same place. Ran it more than once? Pick the run you care about. Anyone who unsubscribed stays marked so you don’t message them again.
</Update>

  ## Unsubscribe & preferences

  Messages can now include an **unsubscribe URL**. Your contact opens a hosted Heyy preferences page and opts out of future marketing messages sent from campaigns and journeys. Unsubscribed contacts show up clearly in the inbox and in campaign views, so they won’t get the next send.

  You handle the relationship and we handle the marketing control. See [Contact subscription](/help/contacts/the-contact/contact-subscription).
</Update>

  AI employees can **add labels**, **remove labels**, and **assign the chat** to users or teams you choose. Tell each one when to act, like “add billing when they ask about invoices.” You can even run assignment automations when the employee assigns, so your current routing still kicks in.

<Update label="August 28, 2026">
  ## AI exit routes on the canvas

  Every exit point on an AI employee now shows up as a **branch on the AI Agent node** in the automation editor. Haven’t set any yet? You’ll see a clear empty state and a link to add them. Already have them? Connect each branch the way you want.

  You can reference exit points in guidelines and prompts just like tools and attributes. When a chat ends, the flow can keep going: assign, tag, send, wait. See [Use exit points in automations](/help/ai-employees/going-live/use-exit-points-in-automations).
</Update>

<Update label="August 25, 2026">
  ## Override time on date triggers

  Date-reached automations follow a contact’s date or datetime attribute. With datetime fields, the stored time used to win every time. A birthday saved as `1992-03-12 03:47:18` could trigger a 3:47am WhatsApp nobody wanted.

  You can now **override the time**: keep the date from the attribute, pick when it fires in your workspace timezone. Birthdays at 10:00. Renewals at 9:00.
</Update>

<Update label="August 22, 2026">
  ## Customize the contact card

  You can **show and hide sections** on the contact card and pin what matters (chat data in a thread, contact data on the profile). Labels also get their own dialog instead of being crammed into a corner.

  Open a chat, see what you need, scroll less. See [Contact card](/help/inbox/contact-card).
</Update>

<Update label="August 19, 2026">
  ## Cancel a message

  **Delete scheduled message** from the inbox while you still can. Wrong thread, wrong timing, variable still blank. Pair it with scheduled sends and that 6pm “quick send” is less likely to become a 6:01 panic message.

  Fewer “sorry, ignore that” follow-ups.
</Update>

<Update label="August 15, 2026">
  ## Undo, redo, and tidy up

  Automations now have **undo**, **redo**, and **tidy up**. Dragged a node somewhere weird? Undo. Layout got messy? Tidy. Serious workflows, less fragile editing.
</Update>

<Update label="August 12, 2026">
  ## Heyy on mobile

  Heyy on mobile finally feels built for a phone. There’s a **bottom bar**, sub-navigation, and a user menu that match how you actually move between inbox, the rest of the product, and your account. Chat list, chat view, and header are tuned for thumbs, not a shrunk desktop.

  You probably won’t build a 40-action long automation on the train. You can answer, assign, and keep a conversation going when the laptop stayed at the office.
</Update>

<Update label="August 11, 2026">
  ## AI that pauses like a person

  Set a **response delay** with a minimum and maximum, and we’ll pick something random in between before the employee replies. On supported channels, typing indicators still show while they’re composing.

  Playground stays instant for testing. Live chats can feel like someone read the message and thought for a beat, not like a webhook fired. See [Configure employee settings](/help/ai-employees/capabilities/employee-settings).
</Update>

<Update label="August 8, 2026">
  ## Send contact cards on WhatsApp

  From the inbox or a **Send contacts** automation step, share one or more WhatsApp contact cards with first name, last name, business name, and phone. Add another, edit, send (up to WhatsApp’s limit per message).

  Great for intros, field teams, or “talk to this person” without a phone number buried in a text bubble.
</Update>

<Update label="August 7, 2026">
  ## Fix an AI reply from the inbox

  Use **Fix response** on a message: we grab the customer’s question (and tell you if we can’t), you write the right answer, and it saves as Q&A knowledge for that employee. Original mistake and correction stay in context, right in the thread.

  The next customer with the same question gets the better answer. See [Build a Q&A knowledge base](/help/ai-employees/content/build-qa-knowledge-base).
</Update>

<Update label="August 7, 2026">
  ## Chat history that tells the story

  The inbox now logs **system messages** for opens, closes, snoozes, assignments (and unassignments), plus when an AI exit route fired. You’ll see who did it (teammate, automation, API, system, or AI employee) and when.

  Handovers and audits get a lot easier to follow.
</Update>

<Update label="August 1, 2026">
  ## You’re offline. We’ll say so.

  We now detect when you’re **offline** and when the connection comes back, and we tell you. Café wifi dropping used to feel like Heyy broke. Now it’s obvious it’s the network, not your data.
</Update>

    - **[Reopened chat](/help/automations/triggers/reopened-chat)**: starts when a previously closed conversation gets a new message. Use this for “welcome back” moments, re-assigning or routing returning chats, or triggering a different flow so you don’t repeat the same first-time welcome every time.
    - **[Chat moved](/help/automations/triggers/chat-moved)**: starts when a chat is moved into one of your selected inboxes. Use this to react to routing decisions—like notifying the right team, kicking off a new checklist, or updating what happens next based on where the chat landed.
